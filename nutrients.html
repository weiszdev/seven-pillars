Core Nutrient Sufficiency

Fill measured gaps rather than taking everything. Test first, then supplement with your team's approval.

Cost: Low–Moderate · Large RCT (vitamin D, omega-3); others not gathered

Free & low-cost actions

  • Ask for a vitamin D blood test before choosing a dose.
  • Fish twice a week covers omega-3 for many people.
  • Fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut) are a low-cost probiotic option.
  • Frozen vegetables and fruit instead of greens powders.
  • A store-brand multivitamin if your diet has gaps.

What the research says

  • Large RCT
    VITAL RCT, 25,871 adults 50+: vitamin D 2,000 IU/day reduced confirmed autoimmune disease (HR 0.78); omega-3 HR 0.85, not significant. PubMed 35082139
  • Large RCT
    VITAL: vitamin D did not reduce cancer cases (HR 0.96). Cancer deaths HR 0.83 (0.67–1.02), 0.75 after excluding the first two years (secondary analysis). PubMed 30415629
  • Observational, within a trial
    SWOG S0221: antioxidant supplement use during breast-cancer chemotherapy was associated with higher recurrence risk. PubMed 31855498

Experiential evidence

Experiential / case-based

Radical Remission factors linked to this pillar:

  • Using herbs and supplements

Dr. Kelly A. Turner studied more than 1,000 cases of unexpected ("radical") remission and found these factors came up again and again. Patterns reported by people who experienced remission are valuable for hypotheses and hope, but there's no comparison group, so they can't show cause or how often they work.

Source: Kelly A. Turner, PhD — Radical Remission (2014) and Radical Hope (2020); radicalremission.com

Honest limits

5,000 IU vitamin D is well above the 2,000 IU tested in VITAL, and people whose levels are already high may not need more. Probiotics may be a concern when immunity is very low. Supplements can interact with treatment.
