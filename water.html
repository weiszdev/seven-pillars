Clean Water & Fewer Toxins

Know what is in your water, then filter for that specific thing with a certified filter.

Cost: Free–Low · Exposure-specific (no outcome trials of filtering safe water)

Free & low-cost actions

  • Free: read your utility's annual Consumer Confidence Report (water quality report).
  • Well owners: many state health departments offer low-cost testing.
  • Low cost: a pitcher or faucet filter certified to the NSF/ANSI standard for your contaminant (e.g., lead, PFAS).
  • If pipes may contain lead, run cold water before drinking; never use hot tap water for cooking.
  • Free: ventilate, fix moisture and mold, avoid heating food in plastic.

What the research says

  • Regulatory / exposure science
    Lead, arsenic, nitrate and some PFAS have documented harms at certain levels (EPA drinking-water standards).

Theoretical / emerging ideas: the "new biology of water"

Theoretical / mechanistic: no human outcome studies test this model

In Cancer and the New Biology of Water (Chelsea Green, 2019), Thomas Cowan, MD, proposes that cancer reflects a disturbance of the structured, gel-like water inside cells, drawing on Gerald Pollack's "exclusion zone" (EZ, or "fourth phase") water research. He discusses approaches such as mistletoe (Iscador), structured water, and diet. Pollack's EZ findings themselves are debated; a 2020 critical review favors other explanations.

Mistletoe has its own clinical trial record, with mixed results. In the MISTRAL randomized trial in advanced pancreatic cancer (n=290), it did not improve overall survival (HR 1.13, 0.89–1.44) or quality of life (PubMed 38915151).

Background: Dr. Cowan surrendered his California medical license in December 2020 (accepted January 2021), while on probation since 2017 (California DCA license record).

Source: Chelsea Green book page

Honest limits

There is no trial showing that filtering already-safe water improves cancer or autoimmune outcomes, so we give no effect size here on purpose.
