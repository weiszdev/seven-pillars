Whole-Food Nutrition

Mostly plants, beans, whole grains, fish, olive oil and nuts; fewer ultra-processed foods. Organic where the budget allows.

Cost: Low · One small RCT subgroup + observational cohorts

▶ Video placeholder — Episode 1: Whole-Food Nutrition
Script: scripts.md (episode 1)

Free & low-cost actions

  • Cook dried beans and lentils in bulk; use frozen vegetables and fruit (just as nutritious, cheaper, less waste).
  • Oats, eggs, canned sardines or salmon, seasonal produce.
  • If buying organic, prioritize the produce you eat most; wash all produce.
  • Cut back on sugary drinks and packaged, ultra-processed snacks.

What the research says

  • Small RCT subgroup
    PREDIMED randomized trial subgroup: Mediterranean diet + extra-virgin olive oil, invasive breast cancer HR 0.32 (0.13–0.79). Only 35 cases; a secondary outcome. PubMed 26365989
  • Observational
    NutriNet-Santé cohort, 68,946 adults: highest vs lowest organic-food score, cancer HR 0.75 (0.63–0.88). Absolute difference small; organic buyers tend to be healthier overall. PubMed 30422212

Experiential evidence

Experiential / case-based

Radical Remission factors linked to this pillar:

  • Radically changing your diet

Dr. Kelly A. Turner studied more than 1,000 cases of unexpected ("radical") remission and found these factors came up again and again. Patterns reported by people who experienced remission are valuable for hypotheses and hope, but there's no comparison group, so they can't show cause or how often they work.

Source: Kelly A. Turner, PhD — Radical Remission (2014) and Radical Hope (2020); radicalremission.com

Experiential evidence: Chris Wark (Chris Beat Cancer)

Experiential / case-based

Chris Wark was diagnosed with stage IIIC colon cancer in December 2003 at age 26. He had surgery, then declined the recommended chemotherapy. He adopted a raw vegan diet with about 64 oz of fresh vegetable juice a day and a giant salad daily, later adding some cooked food and fish. He also leaned on his Christian faith and prayer and on other lifestyle changes. He has written Chris Beat Cancer (2018), runs the Square One program, and shares many survivor stories.

Honest limits: this is one survivor story plus self-selected testimonials, with no comparison group. Surgery alone cures a meaningful share of stage III colon cancers (see the surgery-only arm of Moertel 1990), so the diet's role can't be separated out. Declining recommended treatment is a decision to make with your oncologist.

Source: chrisbeatcancer.com (FAQ)

Honest limits

No strong autoimmune outcome trials were gathered here. The organic finding may partly reflect lifestyle, income and activity.
